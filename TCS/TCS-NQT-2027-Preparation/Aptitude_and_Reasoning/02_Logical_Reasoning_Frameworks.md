# Logical Reasoning: Frameworks and Templates

Logical reasoning questions test your ability to understand complex relationships, identify patterns, and draw valid conclusions. The key is to avoid solving in your head. Always draw diagrams or use tables.

---

## 1. Blood Relations

Blood relation problems are notorious for confusing language ("He is the son of the only brother of my mother").

### The Family Tree Framework
Always draw a family tree using a standard notation:
- **Square [ ]**: Male
- **Circle ( )**: Female
- **Horizontal Line (—)**: Siblings (Brother/Sister)
- **Double Horizontal Line (===)**: Married Couple (Husband/Wife)
- **Vertical Line (|)**: Parent to Child generation gap.

*Example:* "A is the brother of B, B is the sister of C, and C is the father of D."
Draw: `[A] — (B) — [C]`
And a line down from `[C]` to `D` (gender unknown).

### The "Pointing to a Photograph" Trick
Break the sentence down starting from the word "My".
*Example:* Pointing to a man, a woman says, "His mother is the only daughter of my mother."
1. "my mother": The woman's mother.
2. "the only daughter of my mother": The woman herself.
3. Therefore, "His mother is [the woman herself]".
4. The woman is the man's mother.

## 2. Seating Arrangements

These can be linear or circular. The biggest mistake is assuming a position too early.

### Circular Arrangement Rules
1. **Draw a Circle with N marks:** Don't just place people randomly. Draw a circle and mark `N` evenly spaced dashes.
2. **Facing Center:**
   - "Left of X" means going **Clockwise**.
   - "Right of X" means going **Anti-Clockwise**.
3. **Facing Outside:**
   - "Left of X" means **Anti-Clockwise**.
   - "Right of X" means **Clockwise**.
4. **Anchor Point:** Start with the most definitive statement (e.g., "A sits opposite to B", or "C is exactly between D and E"). Do not start with negative statements ("A is not next to B").
5. **Cases:** If there are two possibilities for a position, draw two small circles and solve them simultaneously until one contradicts a rule.

## 3. Syllogism

Syllogisms (e.g., "Some cats are dogs. All dogs are birds.") should ALWAYS be solved using **Venn Diagrams**.

### Standard Venn Diagram Templates
1. **"All A are B"**: Draw a small circle A completely inside a larger circle B.
2. **"No A is B"**: Draw two separate circles A and B with a line connecting them crossed out (X).
3. **"Some A are B"**: Draw two intersecting circles A and B. The overlap is the "Some" part.
4. **"Some A are not B"**: Draw circle A intersecting B, but shade a region of A that is outside B.

**Golden Rule:** If a conclusion *could* be false in any valid drawing of the Venn diagram, then the conclusion does NOT follow. Only conclusions that are 100% true in ALL possible drawings are valid.

## 4. Coding and Decoding

These questions require you to find the pattern mapping one word to another.

### Pattern Framework
Write the letters of the alphabet and their corresponding numbers (A=1, B=2 ... Z=26) on your rough sheet immediately when the exam starts. Also, write them forwards and backwards to easily see reverse pairs (A-Z, B-Y, C-X).

Common patterns to check, in order:
1. **Direct Shift:** +1, -1, +2, -2 to the ASCII value of the letter (e.g., CAT -> DBU).
2. **Reverse Order:** The word is spelled backward, or the coded word is the reverse of a shifted word.
3. **Opposite Letter:** A becomes Z, B becomes Y (sum of their numerical positions is always 27).
4. **Vowel/Consonant Logic:** Vowels are shifted by one rule (+1), consonants by another (-1).
5. **Cross Pattern:** The first half of the word is coded differently than the second half, or they swap positions.

## 5. Direction Sense

Always draw a tiny compass rose on your paper (N up, S down, E right, W left).
- **Right Turn:** 90 degrees Clockwise.
- **Left Turn:** 90 degrees Anti-Clockwise.
- **Pythagoras Theorem:** Often needed to find the shortest distance between the starting and ending points: $c^2 = a^2 + b^2$.
